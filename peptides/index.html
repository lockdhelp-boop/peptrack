Compound reference

Timing changes everything.

A compound that clears in eight hours needs a completely different record from one that takes five weeks. These pages cover half-life, activity window, and what each timescale means for keeping a log that is actually worth reading later. Organization only — no dosing or medical advice.

Reference, not recommendation. Listing a compound here is not a claim that it is safe, effective, or approved for any use. These pages describe published pharmacokinetic estimates and recordkeeping only.

Healing · 1.5 hr

BPC-157

One of the shortest half-lives in the catalogue. Clears within a working day, so nothing carries over — and the clock time you log matters as much as the date.

Read reference → Healing · 2 hr

TB-500

A 2-hour half-life against a 48-hour activity window — the clearest case of why half-life alone is a misleading number to track against.

Read reference → GLP-1 · 7 days

Semaglutide

Roughly 35 days to clear, so levels accumulate for over a month before flattening. Also known as Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus.

Read reference → GLP-1 · 5 days

Tirzepatide

Clears in about 25 days against semaglutide's 35, reaching steady state faster. Also known as Mounjaro and Zepbound.

Read reference → Growth hormone · 8 days

CJC-1295 with DAC

The longest half-life in the catalogue — longer than semaglutide. Levels build for weeks, and a missed entry stays visible for over a month.

Read reference → Growth hormone · 30 min

CJC-1295 No DAC

The shortest half-life on the site, and roughly 384× shorter than the DAC version people call by the same name.

Read reference → Growth hormone · 2 hr

Ipamorelin

Short-acting and pulsatile. Clears within half a day, so the clock time on an entry carries most of the information.

Read reference → Growth hormone · 24 hr

MK-677

Oral, and not actually a peptide. No vial, no syringe math — but a 24-hour half-life means consecutive days overlap.

Read reference → Sexual health · 2.7 hr

PT-141

Activity window roughly four times the half-life, and typically used situationally rather than on a cadence.

GHK-Cu

Used both topically and injected, which are not comparable records. Vials run 50–100 mg, ten times most peptides.
