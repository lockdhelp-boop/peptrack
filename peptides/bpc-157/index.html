BPC-157 Tracker

What BPC-157's timing means for tracking

BPC-157 has one of the shortest half-lives of anything in the catalogue — roughly 1.5 hours, with an estimated activity window around 6 hours and effectively nothing measurable left after about 8. That single fact shapes everything about how a record of it needs to look.

Because it clears within a working day, nothing carries over. Monday's entry has no bearing on Tuesday's levels. There is no accumulation to reason about and no loading period — each administration stands alone.

The practical consequence is that the time of day you record matters as much as the date. With a weekly compound, logging a day late barely changes the picture. With a 1.5-hour half-life, an entry recorded at 8am and one at 8pm describe completely different days. If your log only captures dates, it is throwing away most of the signal.

What to capture in the log

  • Record the clock time, not just the date — with this half-life, time of day is the majority of the information.
  • If a routine splits into more than one entry per day, log each one separately rather than summing them.
  • Note the site if you rotate, since nothing about the previous day's entry constrains today's.

Worked reconstitution example

Purely as arithmetic, using round numbers rather than a recommendation. If a 5 mg vial were mixed with 2 mL of bacteriostatic water, the concentration would be 2.5 mg/mL. Measuring 250 mcg from that would come to:

10 units on a U-100 syringe

5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL  →  250 mcg = 0.1 mL = 10 units

Change either the vial size or the water and this number changes completely. Run your own figures in the reconstitution calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the half-life of BPC-157?

Commonly cited at roughly 1.5 hours, with an estimated activity window of about 6 hours. At five half-lives it is essentially cleared within around 8 hours.

Does BPC-157 build up over time?

No. With a half-life measured in hours it clears well within a day, so levels do not accumulate between entries the way a weekly compound does.

How many units is 250 mcg of BPC-157?

It depends entirely on how the vial was mixed. A 5 mg vial in 2 mL of bacteriostatic water gives 25 mcg per unit, so 250 mcg is 10 units on a U-100 syringe. The same vial in 1 mL would make it 5 units.
