What TB-500's timing means for tracking

TB-500 is the clearest example in the whole catalogue of why half-life alone is a misleading number. Its half-life is about 2 hours — but its estimated activity window is around 48 hours, more than twenty times longer.

Those two figures measure different things. Half-life describes how quickly the compound itself leaves. The activity window describes how long the response it sets in motion persists. For TB-500 the compound is gone by lunchtime while what it triggered is still running two days later.

This matters for tracking in a specific way: a chart drawn only on half-life would show almost nothing — a spike and an immediate collapse, every time, with no relationship between entries. That would be actively misleading. It is why PepTrack models the activity window alongside the half-life rather than plotting decay on its own.

What to capture in the log

  • Date matters more than clock time here — the activity window spans days, so an hour either way changes little.
  • Gaps are the useful signal. With a ~48-hour window, the interval between entries says more than any single entry does.
  • Don't read a flat half-life curve as 'nothing is happening' — the two measures diverge more for this compound than almost any other.

Worked reconstitution example

Purely as arithmetic, using round numbers rather than a recommendation. If a 10 mg vial were mixed with 2 mL of bacteriostatic water, the concentration would be 5 mg/mL. Measuring 2.5 mg from that would come to:

50 units on a U-100 syringe

10 mg ÷ 2 mL = 5 mg/mL  →  2.5 mg = 0.5 mL = 50 units

Change either the vial size or the water and this number changes completely. Run your own figures in the reconstitution calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is the half-life of TB-500?

About 2 hours, but its estimated activity window is roughly 48 hours. The two numbers describe different things and the gap between them is unusually large for this compound.

Why is TB-500's activity window so much longer than its half-life?

Half-life measures how fast the compound clears. The activity window measures how long the downstream response lasts. For TB-500 the effect substantially outlives the molecule.

How many units is 2.5 mg of TB-500?

With a 10 mg vial in 2 mL of bacteriostatic water the concentration is 5 mg/mL, so 2.5 mg is 0.5 mL — 50 units on a U-100 syringe. That is half a 1 mL barrel, which is worth checking before drawing.
