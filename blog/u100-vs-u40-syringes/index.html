U-100 vs U-40 Syringes: The 2.5× Mistake

Two syringes that look the same, with markings that mean different things. This is one of the few measuring errors that is both easy to make and large enough to matter.

Arithmetic, not medical guidance. This article covers measurement and recordkeeping only. It does not recommend compounds, amounts, or schedules. Talk to a qualified healthcare professional about anything medical.

The short answer

On a U-100 syringe, one unit is 0.01 mL. On a U-40 syringe, one unit is 0.025 mL. Drawing "10 units" on a U-40 gives you two and a half times the liquid you would get on a U-100.

What the number actually means

The U-number is not about the syringe's size. It describes the insulin concentration the syringe was calibrated for.

  • U-100 means 100 units per mL. So 100 units fills 1 mL, and one unit is 0.01 mL. This is the standard human insulin syringe and what essentially every peptide calculator, chart, and forum post assumes.
  • U-40 means 40 units per mL. So 40 units fills 1 mL, and one unit is 0.025 mL. These are mostly veterinary.

Both syringes can be the same physical size. The barrel might hold 1 mL either way. What differs is how the barrel is marked.

The ratio is constant, which is what makes the error so clean and so easy to miss. Nothing looks wrong. The plunger sits where you expect. The volume is simply 2.5 times what you intended.

How to tell them apart

Read the barrel. The scale is printed on it — U-100 syringes are marked to 100 (or to 30 or 50 on smaller barrels), U-40 syringes to 40. If the highest number on a 1 mL barrel is 40, it is U-40.

Check the packaging. U-40 is usually labelled explicitly, often alongside veterinary branding.

Colour is not reliable. Cap colours are a manufacturer convention, not a standard, and they vary between suppliers and regions. Do not use colour as your check.

"U-50" is not a thing

This one causes real confusion. A 0.5 mL syringe is not a "U-50" syringe. There is no U-50 concentration standard. A 0.5 mL insulin syringe is almost always a U-100 syringe with a smaller barrel that holds 50 units.

So its markings are U-100 markings — one unit is still 0.01 mL. Only the capacity changed. The same is true of 0.3 mL syringes, which hold 30 units on the same U-100 scale.

If you only have U-40

The arithmetic still works; you just need to use the right divisor. Volume in mL is units divided by the scale — 100 or 40 — and everything downstream follows from that. The syringe unit converter handles both scales and warns you when U-40 is selected, precisely because this is the error worth catching.

The safer habit is simply to use one scale consistently and to check the barrel before drawing, every time, rather than relying on which syringe you think is in the drawer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between U-100 and U-40 syringes?

U-100 is calibrated for 100 units per mL, so one unit is 0.01 mL. U-40 is calibrated for 40 units per mL, so one unit is 0.025 mL. The same unit mark holds 2.5 times more liquid on a U-40.

Is a 0.5 mL syringe a U-50 syringe?

No. U-50 is not a standard concentration. A 0.5 mL insulin syringe is normally a U-100 syringe holding 50 units, so its markings are U-100 markings.

How do I tell which syringe I have?

Read the highest number printed on the barrel. A 1 mL barrel marked to 100 is U-100; one marked to 40 is U-40. Cap colour is a manufacturer convention and is not reliable.

How many mL is 10 units?

0.1 mL on a U-100 syringe, or 0.25 mL on a U-40 syringe.
